The Root Bottleneck

The "Espresso Trap" in Every Busy Cafe

What Happens Every Morning
  • Customers default to lattes, flat whites, and cappuccinos.
  • Each espresso drink takes 120–180 seconds of non-stop grinding, tamping, and steaming.
  • Orders pile up 15–20 tickets deep.
  • Wait times balloon to 10+ minutes and stress spikes.
The Unused Secret
  • Fresh single-origin batch brew and nitro cold brew take less than 20 seconds to pour.
  • They offer equal or higher profit margins.
  • Yet during the rush, these stations sit completely idle because nobody directs traffic.
Understanding the Rush Bottleneck Slide 2 / 7
The Philosophy

Freedom of Choice + Google Maps-Style Guidance

1. Full Freedom

Every item is always available. We never disable drinks or force customers into choices they don't want.

2. Upfront Transparency

Every drink shows estimated wait times upfront (e.g., Ready in ~2m vs Ready in ~8m) so there are no surprises.

3. The 30% Effect

When 3 out of 10 hurried commuters pick the 2-minute cold brew, the espresso backlog clears for everyone else.

Engineered for Reliability

Full-Stack Architecture & Discrete-Event Simulation (DES)

⏱️ Discrete-Event Simulation (DES)

Models real-world store dynamics with SimPy. Every customer is simulated as an asynchronous event token navigating stochastic Poisson arrival intervals, cashier queues, and station server pools to validate queue relief.

🧠 Resilient AI Fallback Ladder

Guarantees zero downtime by cascading across 4 Gemini endpoints: 3.6-flash → 3.1-flash-lite → flash-latest → 3.7-flash, backed by local offline heuristic rules.

☁️ Production Google Cloud Run

Zero hardcoded keys via Secret Manager (GEMINI_API_KEY). Dual TypeScript and Python/FastAPI codebases, container-ready with verification label dev-tutorial=cloud-run-ai-challenge.
